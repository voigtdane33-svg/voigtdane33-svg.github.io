Explore these trusted resources to review key concepts and learn more about energy flow and matter cycling in ecosystems.

Food Chains and Food Webs

This is Khan Academy’s high school biology material. It explains producers, consumers, food chains, food webs, trophic levels, decomposers, and energy transfer using images and examples.

Khan Academy. (n.d.). Food chains & food webs. Khan Academy.

Trophic Levels

This resource explains the 10% rule, trophic levels, energy transfer, producers, and consumers. The language is student-friendly for those who need reading support.

National Geographic Society. (n.d.). Energy flow and the 10% rule. National Geographic Education.

Explore Trophic Levels

Scavengers, Detritivores, and Decomposers

This resource explains the difference between decomposers and detritivores and focuses on how decomposition makes nutrients available to producers. It includes examples: earthworms, termites, and millipedes.

National Geographic Society. (n.d.). Decomposers.

Explore Decomposers

Nutrient Cycles

This website highlights how energy flows through ecosystems, while nutrients are recycled. It introduces carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, and other cycles. It explains how matter moves between living and nonliving parts of an ecosystem.

Khan Academy. (n.d.). Introduction to biogeochemical cycles. Khan Academy.

Nutrient Cycles

OpenStax: Biogeochemical Cycles

This website explains how carbon, nitrogen, and phosphorus cycle through organisms and the environment. It includes diagrams showing how plants, animals, and decomposers help recycle matter in ecosystems.

Fowler, S., Roush, R., & Wise, J. (2013). Biogeochemical cycles. In Concepts of Biology. OpenStax.
